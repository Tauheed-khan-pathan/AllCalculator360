Personal Loan EMI Formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P = Loan amount (principal)  •  r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)  •  n = Number of monthly instalments (tenure in months)

Each EMI has two parts: interest on the outstanding balance, and principal that reduces the balance. Early EMIs are mostly interest; as the balance falls, more of each EMI goes towards principal. That is why the amortisation schedule above shows interest falling and principal rising year by year.

Total payment = EMI × n

Total interest = total payment − loan amount

Worked example: A $20,000 personal loan at 10% annual interest for 5 years (60 months): r = 0.10 ÷ 12 = 0.008333, n = 60, EMI = 20,000 × 0.008333 × (1.008333)60 ÷ ((1.008333)60 − 1) = $424.94 per month. Total payment = $424.94 × 60 = $25,496; total interest = $25,496 − $20,000 = $5,496.

Second example: A ₹5,00,000 personal loan at 11% annual interest for 3 years (36 months): EMI = ₹16,369 per month. Total payment = ₹16,369 × 36 = ₹5,89,297; total interest = ₹5,89,297 − ₹5,00,000 = ₹89,297.

Note: this calculation does not include the processing fee (usually 1–3% of the loan amount) or GST on it — see the FAQ below.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a personal loan?

A personal loan is an unsecured loan you borrow from a bank or lender for almost any purpose — medical bills, a wedding, home renovation, travel or consolidating other debt. Unlike a home or car loan, it is not tied to an asset you buy, so the lender takes no security against it. Because there is no collateral, personal loan interest rates are usually higher than secured loans, and the loan is repaid in fixed monthly EMIs over a fixed tenure, typically 1 to 7 years.

How is the EMI calculated?

The EMI is calculated with the formula EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly instalments. For example, a $20,000 loan at 10% a year for 5 years (60 months) has r = 0.10 ÷ 12 = 0.008333 and an EMI of $424.94 a month. A higher rate or a longer tenure raises the total interest you pay, while a longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI.

Does prepaying a personal loan reduce the total interest?

Yes. Every extra payment you make goes straight towards the principal, so the outstanding balance falls faster and future interest is charged on a smaller amount. That shortens the loan or lowers the remaining EMIs and cuts the total interest you pay. Check with your lender first, though — some banks charge a prepayment or foreclosure fee, usually 2–5% of the outstanding amount, so make sure the interest you save is more than the fee.

Are processing fees included in this calculation?

No. This calculator shows only the EMI, total interest and total payment based on the loan amount, rate and tenure. Banks usually charge a processing fee of 1–3% of the loan amount, often deducted from the amount disbursed, plus GST on the fee — so the money you actually receive is slightly less than the loan amount. Always add the processing fee and any insurance charges to the true cost before comparing loans.

Personal loan vs credit card — which is cheaper?

A personal loan is almost always cheaper. Personal loans typically charge 10–15% a year with a fixed tenure and fixed EMIs, while credit cards charge 36–42% a year on revolving balances. A credit card is convenient for small, short-term spending you can repay within the interest-free period, but for a large expense you will repay over months or years, a personal loan costs far less in interest.

What affects your interest rate?

Your credit score is the biggest factor — a higher score usually means a lower rate — followed by your income and job stability, your existing loans and EMIs, and the loan amount and tenure you choose. Lenders also look at your relationship with the bank and whether you have defaulted before. Improving your credit score, keeping existing EMIs low and comparing offers from several lenders before applying are the simplest ways to get a better rate.
