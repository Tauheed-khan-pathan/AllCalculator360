How to Calculate Income Tax

To calculate income tax by hand for FY 2025-26, start with your gross income and subtract the standard deduction — ₹75,000 under the New Regime or ₹50,000 under the Old Regime — plus any other deductions you claim, such as Section 80C and HRA under the Old Regime. The result is your taxable income. Then apply the slab rates to each slice of that income in turn: only the part inside a slab is taxed at that slab's rate, so a higher income never means the whole income is taxed at the top rate.

After adding up the slab taxes, add 4% cess, then apply the Section 87A rebate if your taxable income is within the limit — up to ₹12,00,000 under the New Regime, ₹5,00,000 under the Old Regime — and marginal relief if you are just above the New Regime limit. Because the two regimes allow different deductions, the only reliable comparison is to work out both, which is what the calculator above does. Your tax also interacts with your savings: the NPS Calculator shows the extra ₹50,000 deduction under Section 80CCD(1B), and the PPF Calculator shows tax-free growth under Section 80C.

Income Tax Formula

New Regime: Taxable income = Gross income − ₹75,000 standard deduction. Slabs on taxable income: ₹0 – ₹4,00,000: Nil; ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000: 5%; ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000: 10%; ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000: 15%; ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000: 20%; ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000: 25%; above ₹24,00,000: 30%. Then add 4% cess on the tax. If taxable income is up to ₹12,00,000, the Section 87A rebate makes the tax zero; just above ₹12,00,000, marginal relief caps the tax at the amount by which taxable income exceeds ₹12,00,000.

Old Regime: Taxable income = Gross income − ₹50,000 standard deduction − Section 80C (up to ₹1,50,000) − Section 80D − HRA exemption. Slabs on taxable income — below 60 years: ₹0 – ₹2,50,000: Nil; ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000: 5%; ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000: 20%; above ₹10,00,000: 30%. Age 60–80 years: ₹0 – ₹3,00,000: Nil; ₹3,00,001 – ₹5,00,000: 5%; ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000: 20%; above ₹10,00,000: 30%. Above 80 years: ₹0 – ₹5,00,000: Nil; ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000: 20%; above ₹10,00,000: 30%. Then add 4% cess on the tax. If taxable income is up to ₹5,00,000, the Section 87A rebate (up to ₹12,500) makes the tax zero.

Income Tax Example

Here is one income worked through both regimes, so you can see exactly where the difference comes from.

Worked example: Gross income ₹15,00,000, no deductions, age below 60. New Regime: taxable income = 15,00,000 − 75,000 = ₹14,25,000; tax = 5% of ₹4,00,000 + 10% of ₹4,00,000 + 15% of ₹2,25,000 = 20,000 + 40,000 + 33,750 = ₹93,750; cess 4% = ₹3,750; total tax = ₹97,500. Old Regime: taxable income = 15,00,000 − 50,000 = ₹14,50,000; tax = 5% of ₹2,50,000 + 20% of ₹5,00,000 + 30% of ₹4,50,000 = 12,500 + 1,00,000 + 1,35,000 = ₹2,47,500; cess 4% = ₹9,900; total tax = ₹2,57,400. The New Regime saves ₹1,59,900.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which regime is better — New or Old?

It depends on your income and deductions. The New Regime has lower slab rates and a higher standard deduction (₹75,000), so it usually wins if you claim few deductions. The Old Regime can win if you claim large deductions under Sections 80C, 80D and HRA. This calculator compares both for your exact numbers, so use the regime it shows as better for you.

What is the Section 87A rebate?

Under the New Regime for FY 2025-26, if your taxable income is up to ₹12,00,000, your tax is fully rebated to zero. Under the Old Regime, if your taxable income is up to ₹5,00,000, you get a rebate of up to ₹12,500, which also brings your tax to zero.

What is marginal relief?

Marginal relief protects you just above the rebate limit. Under the New Regime, if your taxable income is just above ₹12,00,000, your tax (before cess) cannot be more than the amount by which your income exceeds ₹12,00,000. For example, at a taxable income of ₹12,10,000, the tax is capped at ₹10,000 instead of ₹61,500.

Is this calculator's result final?

No — the result is an estimate. It uses the FY 2025-26 slab rates, standard deductions, the Section 87A rebate and marginal relief, and a 4% cess. It does not include surcharge, and your actual tax may differ because of other income, deductions or exemptions. Consult a chartered accountant (CA) before filing your return.

Why is surcharge not included?

Surcharge applies only at higher incomes — above ₹50 lakh — and adds several extra rates and thresholds. It is not included in this version of the calculator, so the result is an estimate, especially for very high incomes.

Do I have to file a return if my tax is zero?

Yes, in many cases. If your gross income is above the basic exemption limit — ₹4,00,000 under the New Regime for FY 2025-26 — you must file a return even if the rebate brings your tax to zero. Filing also helps you claim refunds, carry forward losses and keep a clean record for loans and visas.
