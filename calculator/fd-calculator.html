FD Formula

FV = P × (1 + r/n)(n×t)

P = Deposit  •  r = Annual interest rate ÷ 100  •  n = Compounding periods per year  •  t = Time period in years

Worked example: 100,000 deposited at 7% annual interest, compounded quarterly, for 5 years: r = 0.07, n = 4, t = 5, FV = 100,000 × (1 + 0.07/4)20 = 141,478. Interest earned = 141,478 − 100,000 = 41,478.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an FD?

A fixed deposit (FD) is a savings account where you deposit a lump sum with a bank for a fixed period at a fixed interest rate. Your money stays locked in until maturity, when you get back your deposit plus the interest it earned.

How is FD interest calculated?

This calculator uses the compound-interest formula FV = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t), where P is the deposit, r is the annual rate divided by 100, n is the number of compounding periods per year and t is the number of years. Interest is added to your balance at each compounding period, so it earns interest on interest.

Quarterly vs yearly compounding — which is better?

Quarterly compounding is slightly better, because interest is added four times a year and starts earning interest sooner. For example, 100,000 at 7% for 5 years grows to 141,478 with quarterly compounding and 140,255 with yearly compounding.

Is FD interest taxable?

Yes, in most countries FD interest is taxable income and is added to your total income for the year. In India, for example, banks deduct TDS if the interest in a year crosses the set limit. Tax rules vary by country, so check your local rules.

FD vs savings account — which is better?

An FD usually pays a higher interest rate than a savings account, but your money is locked in for the chosen period. A savings account pays less but lets you withdraw any time. Choose an FD for money you will not need soon, and a savings account for money you may need at short notice.
