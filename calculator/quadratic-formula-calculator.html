How to Calculate Quadratic Formula

To solve a quadratic equation with the quadratic formula, first read off the coefficients a, b and c from the equation ax² + bx + c = 0. Next, work out the discriminant, D = b² − 4ac, which sits under the square root in the formula. Then put the numbers into x = (−b ± √D) ÷ 2a and work out the two cases: one with the plus sign and one with the minus sign. Those two values are the roots — the values of x that make the equation equal to zero.

The discriminant decides what kind of roots you get before you even finish the calculation: a positive discriminant gives two different real roots, zero gives one double root, and a negative discriminant gives two complex roots. If the arithmetic gets heavy, the Scientific Calculator handles the square roots and powers for you.

Quadratic Formula

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a

Discriminant: D = b² − 4ac

Vertex: x = −b ÷ 2a, y = a x² + b x + c

The two roots come from the ± sign: x₁ = (−b + √D) ÷ 2a and x₂ = (−b − √D) ÷ 2a. When D is negative, √D is imaginary, so the roots are complex numbers of the form p ± qi.

Quadratic Formula Example

Worked example: Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0. Here a = 1, b = −5 and c = 6. The discriminant is D = (−5)² − 4 × 1 × 6 = 25 − 24 = 1. Then x = (5 ± √1) ÷ 2, so x = (5 + 1) ÷ 2 = 3 and x = (5 − 1) ÷ 2 = 2. The vertex is at x = 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5, and y = 2.5² − 5 × 2.5 + 6 = −0.25.

Worked example: Solve x² + 2x + 5 = 0. Here a = 1, b = 2 and c = 5. The discriminant is D = 2² − 4 × 1 × 5 = 4 − 20 = −16. Because D is negative, the roots are complex: x = (−2 ± √−16) ÷ 2 = (−2 ± 4i) ÷ 2, so the roots are −1 + 2i and −1 − 2i. The vertex is at (−1, 4).

Worked example: Solve x² − 4x + 4 = 0. Here a = 1, b = −4 and c = 4. The discriminant is D = (−4)² − 4 × 1 × 4 = 16 − 16 = 0. There is one double root: x = (4 ± 0) ÷ 2 = 2.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the quadratic formula?

The quadratic formula solves any quadratic equation ax² + bx + c = 0: x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a. Plug in the values of a, b and c, and it gives you the roots — the values of x that make the equation true. For x² - 5x + 6 = 0, the formula gives x = 2 and x = 3.

What does the discriminant tell you?

The discriminant is D = b² - 4ac, the part under the square root in the quadratic formula. If D is positive, there are two different real roots; if D is zero, there is one double root; if D is negative, there are two complex roots. For x² - 5x + 6 = 0, D = 1, so there are two real roots, 2 and 3.

How do you solve x² - 5x + 6 = 0?

Here a = 1, b = -5 and c = 6. The discriminant is D = (-5)² - 4 × 1 × 6 = 25 - 24 = 1. Then x = (5 ± √1) / 2, so x = (5 + 1) / 2 = 3 and x = (5 - 1) / 2 = 2. The roots are 2 and 3. You can check: 2² - 5 × 2 + 6 = 0 and 3² - 5 × 3 + 6 = 0.

What if the discriminant is negative?

Then the equation has no real roots, but it has two complex roots. For x² + 2x + 5 = 0, the discriminant is D = 4 - 20 = -16, so the roots are x = -1 + 2i and x = -1 - 2i, where i is the square root of -1. The calculator shows these complex roots automatically.

What is the vertex of a parabola?

The vertex is the highest or lowest point of the parabola y = ax² + bx + c. Its x-coordinate is x = -b / 2a, and its y-coordinate is found by putting that x back into the equation. For x² - 5x + 6 = 0 the vertex is (2.5, -0.25), the lowest point of the parabola, because a is positive and the parabola opens upwards.

What if a = 0?

If a = 0, the equation is no longer quadratic — the x² term disappears and bx + c = 0 is a linear equation with the single solution x = -c / b. The quadratic formula needs a to be non-zero because it divides by 2a, so the calculator asks you to enter a value other than zero for a.
