How to Calculate NPS

To calculate your NPS corpus by hand, you would simulate the account month by month: add the monthly contribution at the start of each month, then grow the whole balance by the monthly growth factor — 1 plus the expected annual return divided by 100 and by 12. Repeat that for every month until retirement age. The calculator above does exactly this simulation, which is why its result matches the month-by-month method rather than a single compound-interest formula.

At retirement, the corpus splits under the 60/40 rule: up to 60% comes out as a tax-free lump sum, and the remaining 40% buys an annuity that pays a monthly pension for life. The pension is the annuity amount times the annuity rate, divided by 100 and by 12. Because NPS returns are market-linked, the expected return you enter is an assumption, not a guarantee — try a lower return to see the cautious case. For a guaranteed, tax-free comparison, use the PPF Calculator, and for the full retirement picture, the Retirement Calculator.

NPS Formula

Years to retirement = retirement age − current age  •  Months = years × 12

g = 1 + (expected return rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12)  •  each month: balance = (balance + monthly contribution) × g

Years = retirement age − current age  •  g = monthly growth factor  •  PMT = monthly contribution

This calculator simulates month by month, using the same method as the Retirement Calculator. The monthly contribution is added at the start of each month, then the whole balance is multiplied by the monthly growth factor g — this is the annuity-due basis, so each contribution earns growth for that full month. The corpus at retirement is the simulated future value at the end of the last month.

Total contributions = monthly contribution × months

Total growth = corpus − total contributions

Lump sum = 60% of corpus (tax-free)  •  Annuity amount = 40% of corpus

Monthly pension = annuity amount × annuity rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12

At retirement, up to 60% of the corpus can be withdrawn as a tax-free lump sum, and the remaining 40% must be used to buy an annuity that pays a monthly pension for life. The annuity rate is the yearly rate the annuity provider pays on that 40% amount.

NPS Example

Here are two worked examples — a 30-year plan and a shorter 25-year plan.

Worked example: ₹5,000 a month at a 10% expected return for 30 years (age 30 to 60, 360 months): g = 1 + 0.10 ÷ 12 = 1.008333. Simulating month by month, the corpus at retirement is ₹1,13,96,627. Total contributions = ₹5,000 × 360 = ₹18,00,000; total growth = ₹1,13,96,627 − ₹18,00,000 = ₹95,96,627. Lump sum = 60% of ₹1,13,96,627 = ₹68,37,976 (tax-free); annuity amount = 40% of ₹1,13,96,627 = ₹45,58,651. Monthly pension at a 6% annuity rate = ₹45,58,651 × 6 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = ₹22,793 a month. After the first year the balance is ₹63,351, and after 5 years it is ₹3,90,412.

Second example: ₹10,000 a month at a 12% expected return for 25 years grows to a corpus of ₹1,89,76,351.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is NPS?

NPS (National Pension System) is a government-backed retirement savings scheme in India, regulated by the PFRDA and open to Indian citizens aged 18 to 70. You contribute regularly during your working years, the money is invested in market-linked funds, and at retirement it has grown into a corpus that funds your pension.

What is the 60/40 rule at retirement?

At retirement, you can withdraw up to 60% of your NPS corpus as a tax-free lump sum. The remaining 40% must be used to buy an annuity from an Annuity Service Provider, and that annuity pays you a monthly pension for life. This calculator shows both parts: the lump sum and the monthly pension from the 40%.

What is the difference between Tier I and Tier II NPS accounts?

Tier I is the main retirement account: it is locked in until you turn 60 and it is the account that gets the tax benefits. Tier II is an optional, voluntary savings account that you can only open if you have a Tier I account — you can withdraw from it any time, but it gives no tax benefits.

What are the tax benefits of NPS?

Your own contributions qualify for a deduction of up to ₹1.5 lakh a year under Section 80CCD(1), within the overall Section 80C limit, plus an extra ₹50,000 deduction under Section 80CCD(1B) over and above that limit. Your employer's contribution is deductible separately under Section 80CCD(2), up to 10% of your salary. At retirement, the 60% lump sum is tax-free, while the monthly pension from the annuity is taxed as income.

What does the annuity rate mean for my monthly pension?

The annuity rate is the yearly rate the annuity provider pays on the 40% of your corpus that buys the annuity. Your monthly pension is the annuity amount multiplied by the annuity rate, divided by 100 and then by 12. A higher annuity rate means a higher monthly pension for the same corpus — for example, at a 6% annuity rate, a ₹45,58,651 annuity amount pays ₹22,793 a month. The rate is fixed when you buy the annuity and depends on your age, the annuity type and prevailing interest rates.

NPS vs PPF — which is better?

PPF gives guaranteed, fully tax-free returns and lets you withdraw the entire amount at maturity, while NPS is market-linked, can earn more over the long term, but locks 40% of your corpus into an annuity at retirement. PPF suits safe, fully flexible saving; NPS suits building a larger retirement corpus with a guaranteed monthly pension.
