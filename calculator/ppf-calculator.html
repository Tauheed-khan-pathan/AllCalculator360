How to Calculate PPF Maturity Value

PPF, the Public Provident Fund, is a government-backed savings scheme in India with a 15-year lock-in. You deposit a fixed amount every year, interest is compounded annually, and both the deposits and the interest are tax-free under the EEE benefit. Because each yearly contribution is added at the start of the year, it earns interest for that full year — which is why starting early and depositing the maximum allowed each year makes such a difference.

To estimate your maturity value, you need your yearly deposit, the current interest rate and the tenure. The rate is set by the government and revised every quarter, so enter the rate that applies today. If you prefer a lump-sum deposit instead of yearly saving, compare with the FD Calculator, or see how monthly investing compares with the SIP Calculator.

PPF Calculator Formula

Maturity Value = P × [((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r)

Total Deposits = P × n  •  Interest Earned = Maturity Value − Total Deposits

P = yearly contribution  •  r = annual interest rate ÷ 100  •  n = tenure in years

PPF interest is compounded annually, and the contribution is added at the start of each year — this is the annuity-due basis, so each year's contribution earns interest for that full year. The interest in any year equals the opening balance (after that year's contribution) multiplied by the rate: interest in year y = (balance at end of year y − 1 + P) × r.

PPF Example

The worked example below applies the formula above to the maximum yearly deposit over the full 15-year lock-in.

Worked example: ₹1,50,000 a year at 7.1% for 15 years: r = 0.071, n = 15. Maturity Value = 1,50,000 × [((1.071)15 − 1) ÷ 0.071] × 1.071 = 1,50,000 × 27.1214 = ₹40,68,209. Total Deposits = 1,50,000 × 15 = ₹22,50,000; Interest Earned = ₹40,68,209 − ₹22,50,000 = ₹18,18,209. After the first year the balance is ₹1,60,650, and after 5 years it is ₹9,25,701.

Extending the account: if you keep contributing ₹1,50,000 a year at 7.1% and extend to 20 years, the maturity value grows to ₹66,58,288 (total deposits ₹30,00,000, interest earned ₹36,58,288) — five extra years add ₹25,90,079 to the maturity value.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is PPF?

PPF (Public Provident Fund) is a long-term savings scheme backed by the Government of India. It offers EEE tax benefit — the money you deposit, the interest you earn and the maturity amount are all tax-free. You can open a PPF account at a bank or post office, and it is one of the safest ways to save for the long term because the return is guaranteed by the government.

What is the minimum and maximum amount I can deposit in PPF?

You must deposit at least ₹500 in a financial year to keep the account active, and you can deposit up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year. You can deposit in a lump sum or in instalments, but the total in one financial year cannot exceed ₹1,50,000.

What is the lock-in period of PPF?

PPF has a lock-in period of 15 years from the end of the financial year in which you opened the account. After 15 years you can withdraw the full maturity amount, or extend the account in blocks of 5 years — with or without fresh contributions — for as long as you wish.

Can I withdraw money from PPF before maturity?

Yes, partly. You can take a loan against your PPF balance from the 3rd financial year, and you can make a partial withdrawal from the 7th financial year onwards, subject to limits based on your balance. Full withdrawal is allowed only after the 15-year lock-in ends.

Can the PPF interest rate change?

Yes. The PPF interest rate is set by the Government of India and is revised every quarter. The current rate is 7.1% per annum (as of November 2026), but it can go up or down in future revisions, so the maturity value shown here is an estimate based on the rate you enter.

Is PPF better than FD?

It depends on your goal. PPF usually pays a higher rate than most fixed deposits, its interest and maturity amount are fully tax-free, and it is backed by the government — but your money is locked in for 15 years. A fixed deposit is better when you may need the money sooner or want a fixed rate for a short tenure. Many savers use both: PPF for long-term tax-free growth and an FD for money they may need sooner.
