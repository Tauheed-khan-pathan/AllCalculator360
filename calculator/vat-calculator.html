How to Calculate VAT

To calculate VAT by hand, you need the price and the VAT rate. To add VAT, multiply the price by the rate and divide by 100 — that is the VAT amount — then add it to the price. To remove VAT from a price that already includes it, divide the gross price by (1 + rate ÷ 100) to get the net price, then subtract the net price from the gross price to get the VAT.

VAT works the same way as other consumption taxes, but the rates and rules are different in every country — the UK charges 20% on most goods and services, while many EU countries charge between 19% and 27%. If you are working with Indian tax instead, use the GST Calculator, and for US-style tax use the Sales Tax Calculator.

VAT Formula

Add VAT: VAT = Net Amount × rate ÷ 100  •  Gross = Net + VAT

Remove VAT: Net = Gross Price ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100)  •  VAT = Gross Price − Net

Amounts are rounded to two decimal places (to the penny) after each step, so the net amount plus the VAT always adds up to the gross amount exactly.

VAT Example

Here are three worked examples — adding VAT at the standard rate, removing it again, and adding VAT at the reduced rate.

Worked example — Add VAT: £100 net at 20% VAT: VAT = 100 × 20 ÷ 100 = £20, Gross = 100 + 20 = £120.

Worked example — Remove VAT: £120 gross at 20% VAT: Net = 120 ÷ 1.20 = £100, VAT = 120 − 100 = £20.

Worked example — Reduced rate: £200 net at 5% VAT: VAT = 200 × 5 ÷ 100 = £10, Gross = 200 + 10 = £210.

VAT rates differ by country: the UK standard rate is 20%, with a 5% reduced rate and a 0% zero rate for some goods. Many EU countries charge standard rates between 19% and 27% — Germany 19%, France 20%, Italy 22% and Hungary 27% — and some countries outside Europe charge much lower rates or no VAT at all. Frequently Asked Questions

What is VAT?

VAT (Value Added Tax) is a consumption tax added to the price of most goods and services in the UK, the EU and many other countries. It is charged at each stage of production and sale, but the cost is ultimately paid by the final buyer — businesses reclaim the VAT they pay and pass the rest on.

How do I add VAT to a price?

Multiply the price by the VAT rate and divide by 100 to get the VAT amount, then add it to the price. For example, on a net price of £100 at 20% VAT, the VAT is 100 × 20 ÷ 100 = £20, so the gross price is £120.

How do I remove VAT from a price?

Divide the gross price by (1 + rate ÷ 100) to get the net price, then subtract it from the gross price to get the VAT. For example, £120 gross at 20% VAT gives a net price of 120 ÷ 1.20 = £100, so the VAT is £20.

What is the VAT rate in the UK?

The UK standard rate is 20% and applies to most goods and services. A reduced rate of 5% applies to things like domestic fuel and power and energy-saving materials, and a zero rate of 0% applies to most food, books and children's clothing.

What VAT rates do other countries charge?

VAT rates differ by country. Most EU countries charge a standard rate between 19% and 27% — Germany 19%, France 20%, Italy 22% and Hungary 27% — while some countries charge much lower rates or no VAT at all. Use the custom rate option above to work with any country's rate.

What is the difference between VAT and GST?

VAT and GST are both consumption taxes, but they belong to different systems. VAT is used in the UK, the EU and many other countries, while GST is India's single national tax, introduced in 2017 to replace India's earlier VAT, excise duty and service tax. The maths is the same — add or remove a percentage — but the rates and rules are different.
