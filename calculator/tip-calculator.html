Tip Calculator Formula

Tip = bill amount × tip % ÷ 100

Total = bill amount + tip

Per-person share = total ÷ number of people  •  Tip per person = tip ÷ number of people

Tip is the amount you add on top of the bill for service. Total is what the table pays altogether, and dividing it by the number of people gives each person's fair share. All money amounts on this page are shown to 2 decimal places, so cents are never lost.

Worked example: A $100 bill with a 15% tip, split between 2 people: tip = $100 × 15 ÷ 100 = $15.00; total = $100 + $15.00 = $115.00; each person pays $115.00 ÷ 2 = $57.50, of which $7.50 is tip ($15.00 ÷ 2).

Second example: An $85.50 bill with a 20% tip, split between 3 people: tip = $85.50 × 20 ÷ 100 = $17.10; total = $85.50 + $17.10 = $102.60; each person pays $102.60 ÷ 3 = $34.20, of which $5.70 is tip ($17.10 ÷ 3).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I tip?

15–20% is standard in the US, and 10% is common in many other countries. Tipping customs vary a lot by country and by the type of service, so check local customs before you tip — in some places, such as Japan, tipping is not expected at all.

Should I tip on the total including tax?

Usually, tip on the pre-tax amount — that is, the bill before sales tax or VAT is added. Tipping on the total including tax means you tip on the tax too, which most people avoid.

How do I split a bill fairly?

The simplest fair way is to divide the total evenly between everyone at the table — this calculator does exactly that. If people ordered very different things, split by what each person ordered instead: add up each person's items, work out the tip on each share, and pay your own share.

Do I tip for takeaway or delivery?

A small tip for delivery is customary, because the driver brings the food to your door. Tipping for takeaway is optional — there is no table service — but many people leave a small amount if the order was large or ready quickly.

What if the service was bad?

A small tip, or speaking to the manager about what went wrong, beats leaving nothing without a word. Leaving no tip at all says nothing about what was wrong, while a word with the manager gives the restaurant a chance to fix it.
