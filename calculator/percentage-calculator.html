Percentage Formulas

X% of Y = (X ÷ 100) × Y

Worked example: 20% of 500 = (20 ÷ 100) × 500 = 0.2 × 500 = 100.

X as % of Y = (X ÷ Y) × 100

Worked example: 25 as a percentage of 200 = (25 ÷ 200) × 100 = 12.5%.

% change = ((Y − X) ÷ X) × 100

Worked examples: 50 → 75 = ((75 − 50) ÷ 50) × 100 = +50% increase. 80 → 60 = ((60 − 80) ÷ 80) × 100 = −25% decrease.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a percentage?

A percentage is a way of expressing a number as a fraction of 100. For example, 20% means 20 out of every 100, or 20 ÷ 100 = 0.2. The word comes from the Latin 'per centum', meaning 'by the hundred'.

How do I find X% of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For example, to find 20% of 500: (20 ÷ 100) × 500 = 100. You can also convert the percentage to a decimal first — 20% = 0.2 — and multiply: 0.2 × 500 = 100.

How do I calculate percentage increase?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide the result by the old value, and multiply by 100. For example, a price rising from 50 to 75 is an increase of (75 − 50) ÷ 50 × 100 = 50%.

How do I calculate percentage decrease?

Subtract the new value from the old value, divide the result by the old value, and multiply by 100. For example, a price falling from 80 to 60 is a decrease of (80 − 60) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%.

What is the difference between a percent and a percentage point?

A percent is a relative change, while a percentage point is the simple difference between two percentages. For example, if an interest rate rises from 5% to 7%, it has risen by 2 percentage points, but by 40% in relative terms ((7 − 5) ÷ 5 × 100).
