How to Calculate Standard Deviation

To calculate standard deviation by hand, first find the mean of your numbers, then work out how far each number sits from that mean. Subtract the mean from each value, square each result so that negative and positive deviations do not cancel out, add all the squared deviations together, and divide by the number of values (for a population) or by one less than the number of values (for a sample). The last step is to take the square root, which brings the answer back into the same units as your original numbers.

The mean itself is simply the sum of the numbers divided by how many there are — the Average Calculator finds it for you if you only need the mean, median and mode. Standard deviation then tells you how tightly the numbers cluster around that mean: a small value means most numbers sit close to the mean, and a large value means they are widely spread.

Standard Deviation Formula

Population SD (σ) = √(Σ(x − μ)² ÷ N)

Sample SD (s) = √(Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (n − 1))

Variance = Σ(x − mean)² ÷ N (population) or ÷ (n − 1) (sample)

Here x is each value, μ (or x̄) is the mean, N is the number of values in the whole population, and n is the number of values in a sample. Variance is the same calculation without the final square root, so standard deviation is simply the square root of the variance.

Standard Deviation Example

Worked example: Take the numbers 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9. The sum is 40 and there are 8 values, so the mean is 40 ÷ 8 = 5. The deviations from the mean are −3, −1, −1, −1, 0, 0, 2, 4, and squaring them gives 9, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 4, 16, which add up to 32. Population variance is 32 ÷ 8 = 4, so population SD is √4 = 2. Sample variance is 32 ÷ 7 = 4.5714, so sample SD is √4.5714 = 2.1381.

Worked example: Take the numbers 10, 20, 30. The mean is 60 ÷ 3 = 20. The squared deviations are 100, 0 and 100, which add up to 200. Population variance is 200 ÷ 3 = 66.6667, so population SD is √66.6667 = 8.165. Sample variance is 200 ÷ 2 = 100, so sample SD is √100 = 10.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is standard deviation?

Standard deviation is a measure of how spread out a set of numbers is around its mean. A small standard deviation means the numbers are close to the mean, while a large one means they are spread far apart. For example, the numbers 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 have a mean of 5 and a population standard deviation of 2.

What is the difference between population and sample standard deviation?

Population standard deviation divides the sum of squared deviations by N, the number of values, and is used when your data is the whole population. Sample standard deviation divides by n - 1 instead, and is used when your data is only a sample taken from a larger population. For 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 the population standard deviation is 2 and the sample standard deviation is about 2.1381.

How do I calculate standard deviation by hand?

First find the mean, then subtract the mean from each value, square each result, add the squares together, and divide by the number of values (or by n - 1 for a sample). Finally, take the square root. For 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 the mean is 5, the squared deviations add to 32, 32 / 8 = 4, and the square root of 4 is 2, so the population standard deviation is 2.

Why is sample standard deviation divided by n - 1?

Dividing by n - 1 instead of n corrects for the fact that a sample tends to underestimate how spread out the full population is. This correction is called Bessel's correction. It matters most for small samples: for 10, 20, 30 the population standard deviation is about 8.165, but the sample standard deviation is 10.

What is variance?

Variance is the square of the standard deviation: it is the average of the squared deviations from the mean. For 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 the population variance is 4 and the sample variance is about 4.571. Variance is in squared units, while standard deviation is in the same units as your data, which is why standard deviation is easier to interpret.

Can standard deviation be negative?

No. Standard deviation is the square root of a sum of squared numbers, and squares are never negative, so it is always zero or positive. It is zero only when every value in the set is the same — for example, 5, 5, 5 has a standard deviation of 0.
