How to Calculate Body Fat Percentage

Body fat percentage is the share of your weight that is fat, rather than muscle, bone and water. Two people can weigh the same and look very different, because one carries more muscle and the other more fat — that is why body fat percentage tells you more than weight alone, and more than the BMI Calculator, which only uses height and weight and cannot tell muscle from fat. The US Navy method estimates it from tape measurements: your height, your neck, your waist, and your hips if you are female.

The neck measurement matters because it adjusts for your frame size — a bigger neck usually means a bigger frame, so the same waist means less fat. The waist is where most of the signal is: a waist that is large compared with your neck and height means more body fat. Measure in the morning, before eating, with the tape flat against your skin and not pulled tight, and take each measurement twice. Pair the result with your BMR Calculator and Calorie Calculator numbers to plan how much to eat and how much to move.

Body Fat Calculator Formula

Men: body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)) − 450

Women: body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.29579 − 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height)) − 450

All measurements must be in the same unit — centimetres or inches, it makes no difference to the result. Fat mass = weight × body fat % ÷ 100, and lean mass = weight − fat mass.

Categories use the standard ranges for this method. Men: Essential 2–5%, Athletes 6–13%, Fitness 14–17%, Average 18–24%, Obese 25% and above. Women: Essential 10–13%, Athletes 14–20%, Fitness 21–24%, Average 25–31%, Obese 32% and above.

Body Fat Calculator Example

Worked example (man): Height 178 cm, neck 40 cm, waist 85 cm. Waist − neck = 45, log10(45) = 1.6532; log10(178) = 2.2504. Denominator = 1.0324 − 0.19077 × 1.6532 + 0.15456 × 2.2504 = 1.0324 − 0.3154 + 0.3478 = 1.0648. Body fat = 495 ÷ 1.0648 − 450 = 14.9% — the Fitness category. At 80 kg, fat mass = 80 × 0.149 = 11.9 kg and lean mass = 80 − 11.9 = 68.1 kg.

Worked example (woman): Height 165 cm, neck 35 cm, waist 75 cm, hip 95 cm. Waist + hip − neck = 135, log10(135) = 2.1303; log10(165) = 2.2175. Denominator = 1.29579 − 0.35004 × 2.1303 + 0.22100 × 2.2175 = 1.29579 − 0.7457 + 0.4901 = 1.0402. Body fat = 495 ÷ 1.0402 − 450 = 25.9% — the Average category. At 65 kg, fat mass = 65 × 0.259 = 16.8 kg and lean mass = 65 − 16.8 = 48.2 kg.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the US Navy body fat method?

The US Navy method estimates body fat from tape measurements instead of weighing you under water or scanning you. It uses your height and the circumference of your neck and waist — plus hips for women — in a formula developed by the US Navy. It is the most widely used tape-measure method because it needs no special equipment.

How accurate is a body fat calculator?

A tape-measure estimate is usually within 3 to 4 percentage points of the true value when measurements are taken carefully, but it is not as accurate as a DEXA scan or hydrostatic weighing. Small errors in measuring your waist move the result by about a percentage point, so measure twice and take the average. Treat the number as a useful guide, not an exact measurement.

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

For men, 14–17% is the fitness range and 18–24% is average; for women, 21–24% is the fitness range and 25–31% is average. Essential fat — the minimum your body needs — is about 2–5% for men and 10–13% for women. Above 25% for men or 32% for women is classed as obese by this method.

Why do women need a hip measurement?

Women store more fat around the hips, so the female formula adds the hip circumference to the waist and subtracts the neck. Without the hip measurement the formula cannot account for that difference, which is why the male and female formulas are different.

How do I measure my waist and neck correctly?

Measure your neck just below the Adam's apple, with the tape snug but not tight. Measure your waist at the navel, relaxed — do not suck in your stomach. Measure your hips at the widest point. Take each measurement twice and use the average, and always measure in the same place for later comparisons.

Is this medical advice?

No. This calculator gives a tape-measure estimate of body fat percentage for general information only. It is not a medical diagnosis and it is not a substitute for a DEXA scan or advice from a doctor. If you are concerned about your weight or body composition, speak to a qualified health professional.
