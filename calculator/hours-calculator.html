How to Calculate Hours

To work out hours for one day, subtract the start time from the end time and then subtract the break. If the end time is earlier than the start, add 24 hours first — the shift runs overnight. Multiply the hours left by the hourly rate to get the pay. For a week, do the same for each day and add the daily hours together; the weekly pay is the total hours times the hourly rate.

Breaks are treated as unpaid here, which is the usual rule for meal breaks. If your break is paid, enter 0 minutes and the whole span from start to end counts as work. To measure the gap between two clock times without pay, use the Time Calculator, and to count the days between two dates, use the Date Calculator.

Hours Formula

Hours worked = (end time − start time) − break

Pay = hours worked × hourly rate

Weekly hours = hours on Monday + Tuesday + Wednesday + Thursday + Friday + Saturday + Sunday

Hours Example

Here are the two worked examples from the formulas above — first one day, then a full week at the same hours.

Worked example: Start 9:00, end 17:00, 30-minute break, $20 per hour. Time on site is 17:00 − 9:00 = 8 hours; minus the break (30 minutes = 0.5 hours) gives 7.5 hours worked. Pay is 7.5 × $20 = $150.

Worked example: The same day five times a week: 5 × 7.5 = 37.5 hours a week, and pay is 37.5 × $20 = $750 for the week.

How to Use the Hours Calculator

The two tools above cover a single shift and a whole week, and both update as you type.

  1. For one day, enter your start time, end time, break in minutes and hourly rate.
  2. Read your hours worked and total pay in the Result box.
  3. For a week, check each day in the table — Monday to Friday start with your one-day times, and blank days count as days off.
  4. Read your weekly hours and weekly pay in the Result box. To check a plain time gap without pay, try the Time Calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my work hours for one day?

Subtract your start time from your end time, then subtract your break. For example, 9:00 to 17:00 is 8 hours, minus a 30-minute break is 7.5 hours. Enter your times in the one-day tool above and it does the subtraction for you.

How is my daily pay calculated?

Multiply your hours worked by your hourly rate. For example, 7.5 hours at $20 per hour is 7.5 × $20 = $150. Break time is unpaid, so it is subtracted before the pay is worked out.

How do I calculate my weekly hours?

Add up the hours worked on each day of the week. For example, five days of 7.5 hours is 5 × 7.5 = 37.5 hours a week. Enter each day in the weekly table above — days left blank count as zero hours.

How do I calculate my weekly pay?

Multiply your total weekly hours by your hourly rate. For example, 37.5 hours at $20 per hour is 37.5 × $20 = $750. The weekly tool above shows your total hours and total pay together.

Are breaks paid or unpaid?

This calculator treats breaks as unpaid, which is the usual rule for meal breaks — your break minutes are subtracted before your hours and pay are worked out. If your break is paid, enter 0 minutes for the break and the full time from start to end counts as work.

What if I work overnight, past midnight?

Enter your times as normal and the calculator treats an end time earlier than the start time as the next day. For example, 22:00 to 6:00 with a 30-minute break is 7.5 hours, because 8 hours overnight minus 30 minutes is 7.5 hours.
