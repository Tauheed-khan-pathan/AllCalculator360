Mortgage Formula

P&I = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Total monthly payment = P&I + (annual property tax ÷ 12) + (annual home insurance ÷ 12) + (loan amount × PMI rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12)

P = Loan amount (home price − down payment)  •  r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)  •  n = Number of monthly payments (loan term in months)

Worked example: Home price $400,000, down payment 20% = $80,000, so the loan amount P = $320,000. Annual rate 6.5% for 30 years (360 months): r = 0.065 ÷ 12 = 0.0054167, n = 360, P&I = 320,000 × 0.0054167 × (1.0054167)360 ÷ ((1.0054167)360 − 1) = $2,023 per month ($2,022.62 exact). Add property tax $4,800 ÷ 12 = $400, home insurance $1,800 ÷ 12 = $150 and PMI $0: $2,022.62 + $400 + $150 + $0 = $2,573 per month ($2,572.62 exact). Total interest = $2,022.62 × 360 − $320,000 = $408,142. Total cost over 30 years = $2,572.62 × 360 = $926,142.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a mortgage payment made of?

A monthly mortgage payment usually has four parts: principal and interest (P&I), property tax, home insurance, and PMI if your down payment is under 20%. This calculator adds all four, so you see the full monthly cost — not just the loan payment.

How is the principal & interest part calculated?

With the EMI formula: P&I = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount (home price minus down payment), r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly payments. Property tax, home insurance and PMI are then added as simple monthly amounts: annual amount ÷ 12.

What is PMI and when do I pay it?

PMI (private mortgage insurance) protects the lender if you stop paying. Lenders usually charge it when your down payment is under 20% of the home price. It typically costs 0.3%–1.5% of the loan amount per year, and you can usually ask to remove it once you have 20% equity in the home.

Does a bigger down payment lower my monthly payment?

Yes, in two ways: it reduces the loan amount, so principal and interest fall, and a down payment of 20% or more usually removes PMI entirely. On a $400,000 home at 6.5% for 30 years, raising the down payment from 10% to 20% cuts the principal & interest part by about $253 a month.

Is the total cost the same as the total interest?

No. Total interest covers only the interest charged on the loan amount. Total cost adds everything you pay over the loan term: principal and interest, plus property tax, home insurance and PMI. For the worked example below, total interest is $408,142 but the total cost is $926,142.
