How to Calculate Daily Calories

Your daily calorie need — TDEE, or Total Daily Energy Expenditure — is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. BMR is what you burn at complete rest, and the activity factor adds everything else: walking, working, housework and exercise. A desk worker and a builder of the same size can need hundreds of calories apart, even though their BMR is identical.

The factors run from 1.2 for a sedentary life to 1.9 for extra-active days, and picking honestly matters more than picking precisely — most people overestimate how active they are, so start one level lower if you are unsure. Once you have your maintenance figure, weight change is a matter of a steady gap: roughly 500 calories a day below maintenance for about half a kilogram a week, or 250 to 500 above it to gain gradually. Your BMR itself comes from the BMR Calculator, and the BMI Calculator shows whether your weight is in the healthy range for your height.

Calorie Formula

TDEE = BMR × Activity Factor

First find your BMR with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation: men 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5; women the same formula ending in − 161. Then multiply by your activity factor — 1.2 sedentary, 1.375 lightly active, 1.55 moderately active, 1.725 very active, or 1.9 extra active.

Calorie Calculator Example

Worked example: a 30-year-old man, 175 cm, 70 kg, moderately active. BMR = 10 × 70 + 6.25 × 175 − 5 × 30 + 5 = 700 + 1093.75 − 150 + 5 = 1648.75 ≈ 1649 kcal/day. TDEE = 1648.75 × 1.55 = 2555.56 ≈ 2556 kcal/day. His targets: lose weight 2056, mild loss 2306, maintain 2556, mild gain 2806, gain 3056 kcal/day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is TDEE?

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is the total number of calories you burn in a day, including your BMR plus the calories burned through daily activity and exercise.

How is TDEE calculated?

TDEE is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor: 1.2 for sedentary, 1.375 for lightly active, 1.55 for moderately active, 1.725 for very active, and 1.9 for extra active.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

A deficit of about 500 calories per day below your maintenance level usually leads to around 0.5 kg of weight loss per week. A smaller deficit of 250 calories per day is a gentler option.

How many calories should I eat to gain weight?

A surplus of about 250 to 500 calories per day above your maintenance level supports gradual, healthy weight gain, ideally combined with strength training.

Are these calorie numbers exact?

No. They are estimates based on averages, and your real needs can differ by a few hundred calories depending on muscle mass, hormones, and daily routine. Adjust your intake if your weight does not move as expected over two to three weeks.

How many calories should I eat to maintain my weight?

Eat roughly your maintenance calories — your TDEE. For the worked example, a moderately active 30-year-old man, 175 cm and 70 kg, maintains on about 2,556 calories a day. If your weight stays steady for two to three weeks at that intake, you have found your maintenance level.
