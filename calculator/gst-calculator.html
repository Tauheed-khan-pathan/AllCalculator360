How to Calculate GST

To calculate GST by hand, you need the price and the GST rate. To add GST, multiply the price by the rate and divide by 100 — that is the GST amount — then add it to the price. To remove GST from a price that already includes it, divide the inclusive price by (1 + rate ÷ 100) to get the base price, then subtract the base price from the inclusive price to get the GST.

For sales within the same state, the GST splits equally into CGST and SGST — at 18%, that is 9% CGST and 9% SGST. For sales to another state, IGST applies instead at the full rate with no split. Getting the split right matters for invoices and returns, which is why the calculator above shows all three. GST Formula

Add GST: GST = Amount × rate ÷ 100  •  Total = Amount + GST

Remove GST: Base = Inclusive Price ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100)  •  GST = Inclusive Price − Base

CGST and SGST are each half of the GST amount on sales within the same state. On interstate sales, IGST applies instead at the full rate with no split.

GST Example

Here are the two worked examples — adding GST to a price, then removing it.

Worked example — Add GST: ₹10,000 at 18% GST: GST = 10,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = ₹1,800 (CGST ₹900 + SGST ₹900), Total = 10,000 + 1,800 = ₹11,800.

Worked example — Remove GST: ₹11,800 inclusive at 18% GST: Base = 11,800 ÷ 1.18 = ₹10,000, GST = 11,800 − 10,000 = ₹1,800.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is GST?

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a single indirect tax levied on the supply of goods and services in India. It replaced most earlier indirect taxes such as excise duty, service tax and VAT, and applies to almost every product and service you buy.

What are CGST and SGST?

CGST (Central GST) and SGST (State GST) are the two parts of GST charged on sales within the same state. Each is half of the total GST rate — for example, at 18% GST, CGST is 9% and SGST is 9%. IGST (Integrated GST) applies instead on interstate sales: it equals the full rate with no split.

How do I add GST to a price?

Multiply the price by the GST rate and divide by 100 to get the GST amount, then add it to the price. For example, on a price of ₹10,000 at 18% GST, the GST is 10,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = ₹1,800, so the total is ₹11,800.

How do I remove GST from a price?

Divide the inclusive price by (1 + rate ÷ 100) to get the base price, then subtract it from the inclusive price to get the GST. For example, ₹11,800 inclusive at 18% GST gives a base price of 11,800 ÷ 1.18 = ₹10,000, so the GST is ₹1,800.

What happened to the 12% and 28% GST slabs?

The 12% and 28% slabs were merged under GST 2.0 in September 2025. Most goods now fall under 5% or 18%, and a 40% demerit rate applies to a small list of luxury and sin goods such as tobacco and high-end cars.

What is the GST rate on most goods now?

After GST 2.0 in September 2025, most goods fall under the 5% or 18% slabs, with a 0% rate for essentials and a 40% demerit rate for a small list of luxury and sin goods. The exact rate depends on the item, so check the rate for your product before you quote a price or file a return.
