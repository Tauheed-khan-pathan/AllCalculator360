How to Calculate Fraction

To calculate with fractions by hand, the method depends on the operation. For addition and subtraction, first rewrite both fractions with a common denominator — usually the lowest common multiple of the two denominators — then add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator. For multiplication, multiply straight across: numerator times numerator over denominator times denominator. For division, flip the second fraction and multiply instead.

Whatever the operation, the last step is always the same: simplify the answer by dividing the numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor. A fraction such as 6/12 is correct but unfinished until it is simplified to 1/2. If a result is an improper fraction, you can also write it as a mixed number — for example, 15/8 is 1 7/8. Fraction Formula

a/b + c/d = (a × d + c × b) ÷ (b × d), then simplify

To add or subtract fractions, first rewrite them with a common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators. To multiply, multiply straight across — numerator × numerator over denominator × denominator. To divide, flip the second fraction and multiply. Always finish by simplifying with the greatest common divisor.

Fraction Example

Here is how the addition rule works on two everyday fractions.

Worked example: 1/2 + 1/3. The common denominator of 2 and 3 is 6, so 1/2 = 3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6. Then 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6, which is already simplified, and 5 ÷ 6 = 0.8333.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a fraction?

A fraction is a part of a whole, written as one number over another — for example 1/2 means one part out of two equal parts. The top number is the numerator and the bottom number is the denominator.

How do you add fractions with different denominators?

First find a common denominator, usually the lowest common multiple of the two denominators. Rewrite each fraction with that denominator, then add the numerators and keep the denominator the same. For example, 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

How do you simplify a fraction?

Divide the numerator and the denominator by their greatest common divisor (GCD) — the largest number that divides both exactly. For example, 6/8 simplifies to 3/4 because both 6 and 8 divide by 2.

How do you divide fractions?

Flip the second fraction (swap its numerator and denominator) and multiply instead. For example, 3/4 ÷ 2/5 = 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 = 1 7/8.

What is a mixed number?

A mixed number combines a whole number with a fraction, such as 1 7/8. It is another way of writing an improper fraction — one whose numerator is larger than its denominator, like 15/8. Divide the numerator by the denominator to get the whole part, and the remainder becomes the new numerator.

How do you multiply fractions?

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together, then simplify the result. For example, 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12, which simplifies to 1/2. Unlike addition, you do not need a common denominator to multiply fractions.
