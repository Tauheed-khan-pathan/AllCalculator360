E-commerce Profit Formula

Net profit = Selling price − Final product cost − Marketplace fees − Returns cost

Marketplace fees = Referral / commission + Closing or fixed fee + Logistics + GST on fees (India, 18%)

Final product cost = Product cost + Packaging cost + Transport cost  •  Real margin = Net profit ÷ Selling price  •  ROI = Net profit ÷ Final product cost  •  Break-even price = the lowest price at which profit is ₹0 or more  •  Max CPA = the most you can spend on ads per order before losing money (equal to your profit per order)  •  Break-even ROAS = Selling price ÷ Max CPA

Worked example (India): Product cost ₹200 + packaging ₹10 + transport ₹20 = final cost ₹230. Selling price ₹499, Home & Kitchen, 500 g, National zone, 15% returns, no ITC. Amazon India — referral ₹0 (price ≤ ₹1,000), closing fee ₹10, shipping ₹57, GST on fees ₹12.06: total fees ₹79.06, returns cost ₹8.55, profit ₹181.39, real margin 36.4%, break-even price ₹318. Flipkart — commission ₹0 (price ≤ ₹1,000), fixed fee ₹10, shipping ₹55, GST ₹11.70: total fees ₹76.70, returns ₹8.25, profit ₹184.05, margin 36.9%, break-even ₹315. Meesho — commission ₹0, shipping ₹45, GST ₹8.10: total fees ₹53.10, returns ₹6.75, profit ₹209.15, margin 41.9%, break-even ₹290. Meesho wins this order by ₹25.10 over Flipkart. Headline margin is 53.9% on all three — the real margin is what the fees leave you.

Worked example (US): Product cost $20, selling price $29.99, Home & Kitchen, 1 lb, no shipping charged, $4.00 shipping cost, 5% returns. Amazon.com — referral 15% = $4.50, FBA fulfilment $3.66, returns $0.18: profit $1.65, margin 5.5%. eBay — final value fee 13.6% = $4.08, per-order fee $0.40, shipping $4.00, returns $0.20: profit $1.31, margin 4.4%.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do US profit calculators give wrong answers for Indian sellers?

Because they use the US fee stack. Indian marketplaces add 18% GST on every fee, a 0.5% TCS deduction, closing and fixed fees, zone-based shipping and returns/RTO losses of 15–20%. A calculator that ignores those can show a 40% margin where the real margin is under 20%. This calculator models GST on fees, TCS, TDS, closing fees, zone shipping and RTO for India — and switches to the US or UK fee stack when you change the country.

What is the difference between headline margin and real margin?

Headline margin is (selling price − product cost) ÷ selling price — it ignores the marketplace. Real margin is net profit ÷ selling price, after referral or commission, closing fees, shipping, GST on fees and returns. On the worked example below, the headline margin is 53.9% but the real margin on Amazon India is 36.4% — the fees take the rest. Always judge a listing by its real margin.

How is the break-even price calculated?

It is the lowest selling price at which the profit per order is zero or more, found by testing prices one rupee (or dollar) at a time from your product cost upwards. That step-by-step search matters in India because fees jump at price slabs — the closing fee changes at ₹300, ₹500 and ₹1,000, and the referral fee switches on above ₹1,000 — so the break-even price is found exactly, not from a smoothed formula.

Can I claim GST input tax credit on marketplace fees?

If your business is GST-registered and you file returns, the 18% GST charged on marketplace fees is usually available as input tax credit — it is a recoverable tax, not a cost. Tick 'I can claim GST input tax credit (ITC)' and the calculator removes GST on fees from your costs (it still shows in the bank settlement, because the marketplace deducts it and you claim it back later). If you cannot claim ITC, leave it unticked and GST on fees counts as a cost.

Are the fee rates live? How often are they updated?

No — this page is a static calculator, so it cannot fetch live rates from Amazon, Flipkart or eBay. The fee rates are built into the page and stamped with their date (9 Nov 2026). Every rate is editable in the 'Fee rates' section below the results, so you can correct a rate the day a marketplace changes it, and the results update instantly. Always double-check a rate against the marketplace's official fee page before a big pricing decision.
