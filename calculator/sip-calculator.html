SIP Formula

FV = P × (((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i) × (1 + i)

P = Monthly investment  •  i = Monthly return rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)  •  n = Number of months

Worked example: 10,000 invested every month at 12% annual return for 10 years (120 months): i = 0.12 ÷ 12 = 0.01, n = 120, FV = 10,000 × (((1.01)^120 − 1) ÷ 0.01) × 1.01 = 2,323,391. Invested amount = 10,000 × 120 = 1,200,000; estimated returns = 2,323,391 − 1,200,000 = 1,123,391.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SIP?

SIP stands for Systematic Investment Plan: you invest a fixed amount every month in a mutual fund, which buys units at the prevailing price — averaging your purchase cost over time.

How are SIP returns calculated?

This calculator uses the future-value formula FV = P × (((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i) × (1 + i), where P is the monthly investment, i is the monthly return rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months. It assumes a constant return rate.

Are SIP returns guaranteed?

No. SIP returns depend on the market, so the actual return can be higher or lower than the assumed rate. The figure here is an estimate, not a promise.

SIP vs lump sum — which is better?

A lump sum can earn more in a steadily rising market, but a SIP spreads risk across market ups and downs and suits a regular monthly income. The better choice depends on your money and risk comfort.

What happens if I stop my SIP?

You can stop a SIP any time without penalty; the money already invested stays invested and keeps growing until you withdraw it.
