How to Calculate Slope

To calculate the slope between two points, divide the change in y (how far the line rises or falls) by the change in x (how far it runs). A positive slope rises from left to right, a negative slope falls, a slope of zero is a flat horizontal line, and a vertical line has an undefined slope because the change in x is zero. The slope is often written as a fraction, rise over run — the Fraction Calculator can simplify that fraction for you.

Once you know the slope, you can write the whole line as y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b is the y-intercept — the point where the line crosses the y-axis. The angle the line makes with the horizontal is the arctangent of the slope, which you can check on the Scientific Calculator.

Slope Formula

Slope m = (y2 − y1) ÷ (x2 − x1)

Y-intercept b = y1 − m × x1

Line equation: y = mx + b

Distance = √((x2 − x1)² + (y2 − y1)²)

Midpoint = ((x1 + x2) ÷ 2, (y1 + y2) ÷ 2)

Slope Example

Worked example: Points (0, 0) and (4, 2): slope m = (2 − 0) ÷ (4 − 0) = 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5. Y-intercept b = 0 − 0.5 × 0 = 0, so the line is y = 0.5x. Distance = √(16 + 4) = √20 ≈ 4.472. Midpoint = ((0 + 4) ÷ 2, (0 + 2) ÷ 2) = (2, 1). Angle = arctan(0.5) ≈ 26.57°.

Worked example: Points (1, 2) and (5, 6): slope m = (6 − 2) ÷ (5 − 1) = 4 ÷ 4 = 1. Y-intercept b = 2 − 1 × 1 = 1, so the line is y = x + 1. Distance = √(16 + 16) = √32 ≈ 5.657. Midpoint = (3, 4). Angle = arctan(1) = 45°.

Worked example (vertical line): Points (1, 2) and (1, 5): the x-values are the same, so the slope is undefined and the line is x = 1. Distance = 3, midpoint = (1, 3.5), and the angle is 90°. The Scientific Calculator confirms the angle: a vertical line stands at 90° to the horizontal.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you find the slope between two points?

Subtract the y-values and divide by the difference of the x-values: slope = (y2 − y1) ÷ (x2 − x1). For example, the slope between (0, 0) and (4, 2) is (2 − 0) ÷ (4 − 0) = 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5. Enter the two points in the calculator above and it works out the slope, the line equation, the distance and the midpoint instantly.

What is the slope of a vertical line?

A vertical line has an undefined slope, because the x-values are the same and the formula divides by zero: (y2 − y1) ÷ 0 has no answer. For example, the line through (1, 2) and (1, 5) is vertical, its equation is x = 1, the distance between the points is 3 and the midpoint is (1, 3.5). The calculator above shows “undefined” for the slope of a vertical line.

What is the slope of a horizontal line?

Zero. A horizontal line has the same y-value at both points, so y2 − y1 = 0 and the slope is 0 ÷ (x2 − x1) = 0. Its equation is y = a constant, for example y = 3. A slope of zero means the line is flat — it neither rises nor falls.

How do you find the y-intercept from two points?

First find the slope m, then use b = y1 − m × x1. For example, the points (1, 2) and (5, 6) give a slope of (6 − 2) ÷ (5 − 1) = 1, so the y-intercept is 2 − 1 × 1 = 1 and the line is y = x + 1. The calculator above shows the y-intercept and the full equation for any two points.

What is the formula for the distance between two points?

Distance = √((x2 − x1)² + (y2 − y1)²). For example, the distance between (0, 0) and (4, 2) is √(16 + 4) = √20 ≈ 4.472. It is the straight-line distance, worked out with Pythagoras’ theorem. The calculator above shows the distance and the midpoint as well as the slope.

What does the slope tell you about a line?

The slope tells you how steep a line is and whether it rises or falls. A positive slope rises from left to right, a negative slope falls, a slope of zero is flat, and an undefined slope is a vertical line. The bigger the slope’s size, the steeper the line: a slope of 2 is steeper than a slope of 0.5. The angle of the line is the arctangent of the slope — for a slope of 0.5 the angle is about 26.57°.
