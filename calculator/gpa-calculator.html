How to Calculate GPA

To calculate GPA by hand, first convert each letter grade into grade points on the 4.0 scale — A is 4.0, B is 3.0, C is 2.0, D is 1.0 and F is 0.0, with plus and minus grades in between, as listed in the formula section below. Then multiply each grade's points by the credit hours of that course, because a 4-credit course counts four times as much as a 1-credit course.

Add up all the grade points, add up all the credit hours, and divide the first total by the second. The result is your semester GPA, shown to two decimal places. The same method works for a cumulative GPA — just include every course you have taken, or enter your previous GPA and credits in the optional section above. Your GPA feeds directly into what you need on your finals, which the Grade Calculator works out for you.

GPA Formula

Semester GPA = Σ(grade points × credit hours) ÷ Σ credit hours

Cumulative GPA = (previous GPA × previous credits + semester grade points) ÷ (previous credits + semester credits)

Each letter grade is worth a fixed number of grade points on the 4.0 scale: A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B− = 2.7, C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, C− = 1.7, D+ = 1.3, D = 1.0 and F = 0.0. A course worth more credits counts more towards your GPA, so a 4-credit A pulls your average up more than a 1-credit A. GPA is shown to 2 decimal places.

GPA Example

Here are two worked examples — one mixed set of grades, and one perfect record.

Worked example 1: A (3 credits), B+ (4 credits), A− (3 credits) and C (2 credits): grade points = 4.0 × 3 + 3.3 × 4 + 3.7 × 3 + 2.0 × 2 = 12 + 13.2 + 11.1 + 4 = 40.3; total credits = 3 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 12; GPA = 40.3 ÷ 12 = 3.36.

Worked example 2: All A grades across 15 credits: grade points = 4.0 × 15 = 60; GPA = 60 ÷ 15 = 4.00.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good GPA?

On the 4.0 scale, a GPA of 3.5 or higher is generally considered good, and 3.7 or higher is excellent — most honours and competitive graduate programmes look for at least 3.5. A GPA of 3.0 (a B average) is solid and meets the minimum for many jobs and programmes, while anything below 2.0 can put you on academic probation at many schools.

How is GPA calculated?

Each letter grade is worth a number of grade points on the 4.0 scale — A is 4.0, B is 3.0, C is 2.0, D is 1.0 and F is 0.0, with plus and minus grades in between. Multiply each grade's points by the course's credit hours, add up all those grade points, and divide by the total credit hours. For example, an A in a 3-credit course earns 12 grade points.

What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?

An unweighted GPA uses the standard 4.0 scale for every course, so the highest possible GPA is 4.0. A weighted GPA gives extra points for harder courses — for example, an A in an honours or AP course might count as 5.0 — so a weighted GPA can go above 4.0. This calculator gives your unweighted GPA on the 4.0 scale, which is the figure most colleges report.

How do I raise my GPA?

Your GPA is an average, so the fastest way to raise it is to earn high grades in courses worth many credits, because each credit counts. Retaking a course you failed or did poorly in can also help if your school replaces the old grade. Over time, new high grades pull the average up, but the more credits you already have, the slower it moves — a single A has a bigger effect early in your studies.

What does the 4.0 scale mean?

The 4.0 scale is the standard way US schools turn letter grades into a single number. A is 4.0, A− is 3.7, B+ is 3.3, B is 3.0, B− is 2.7, C+ is 2.3, C is 2.0, C− is 1.7, D+ is 1.3, D is 1.0 and F is 0.0. Your GPA is the credit-weighted average of those numbers, so it always falls between 0.0 and 4.0.

Can my GPA go above 4.0?

On the unweighted 4.0 scale used by this calculator, no — 4.0 is the maximum, earned with straight A grades. Some schools use a weighted scale that gives extra points for honours or AP courses, so a weighted GPA can rise above 4.0, sometimes to 5.0. Colleges usually look at both figures, so check which scale your school reports.
