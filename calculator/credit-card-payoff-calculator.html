How to Calculate Credit Card Payoff

Credit card interest is charged on the balance you still owe, month by month. Each month the issuer adds interest equal to the balance times the APR divided by 12, then takes your payment: the interest is paid first, and only what is left over reduces the balance. That is why the balance falls slowly at first — when the balance is high, most of your payment is interest — and faster later, as the balance (and therefore the interest) shrinks. The same reducing-balance idea powers the Loan Calculator and the Personal Loan Calculator.

To work out the payoff by hand, repeat that one-month step until the balance reaches zero and count the months. A fixed payment clears the card much faster than minimum payments, because a minimum payment shrinks as the balance shrinks, so it stays only just above the interest charge for years.

Credit Card Payoff Formula

Monthly interest = balance × APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100

Principal paid = monthly payment − monthly interest

New balance = old balance − principal paid

To pay off by a target date: first count the months n to that date, then the payment needed is P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the balance and r is the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100).

Minimum payment (modelled here): 2% of the current balance each month, with a $25 floor (or the equivalent in your currency). The payment falls as the balance falls, which is exactly why minimum-only payoff takes so long.

Note: this calculation assumes a fixed APR, no new purchases, and no fees — see the FAQ below.

Credit Card Payoff Example

Worked example: A $5,000 balance at 18% APR with a fixed $150 monthly payment. Month 1 interest = $5,000 × 0.18 ÷ 12 = $75.00, so $75.00 of the $150 payment goes to principal and the balance falls to $4,925.00. Repeating that step every month, the card is cleared in 47 months (3 years 11 months), with total interest of $1,983.60 and total paid of $6,983.60.

Minimum payments only: the same $5,000 at 18% APR, paying only the minimum (2% of the balance, at least $25), takes 370 months (over 30 years) and costs $12,327.77 in interest — $10,344.17 more interest and 323 months longer than the fixed $150 plan.

Pay off in 2 years: to clear the same $5,000 at 18% APR in 24 months you need $249.62 a month, with total interest of $990.89 — about half the interest of the $150-a-month plan.

Second example: a $1,000 balance at 24% APR with $100 a month is cleared in 12 months, with total interest of $127.03.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is credit card interest calculated each month?

Each month your card issuer charges interest on the balance you still owe: monthly interest = balance × APR ÷ 12. For example, a $5,000 balance at 18% APR is charged $5,000 × 0.18 ÷ 12 = $75 of interest in the first month. Your payment goes to that interest first, and only what is left reduces the balance.

How long will it take to pay off my credit card?

It depends on your balance, your APR and how much you pay each month. A $5,000 balance at 18% APR with a fixed $150 monthly payment takes 47 months (3 years 11 months) to clear, with $1,983.60 of total interest. Enter your own numbers above — a bigger payment shortens the payoff sharply, because more of each payment goes to principal instead of interest.

Why do minimum payments take so long to pay off a card?

Because a minimum payment is usually only about 2% of the balance, so most of it is swallowed by that month's interest and almost nothing reduces the balance. On a $5,000 balance at 18% APR, paying only the minimum (2% of the balance, at least $25) takes 370 months — over 30 years — and costs $12,327.77 in interest, more than twice the original balance. That is why the calculator shows the minimum-payment comparison: it is the most expensive way to repay a card.

What monthly payment pays off my card in 2 years?

Use the "Pay off by a date" mode above and pick a date 24 months away — the calculator works out the fixed monthly payment for you. For example, a $5,000 balance at 18% APR needs $249.62 a month to be debt-free in 24 months, with $990.89 of total interest — about half the interest of the $150-a-month plan, which takes 47 months.

What if my monthly payment is less than the monthly interest?

Then the balance never falls — the payment does not even cover the interest, so the debt grows instead of shrinking. For example, on a $5,000 balance at 18% APR the first month's interest alone is $75, so any payment of $75 or less can never pay the card off. The calculator warns you when this happens: raise the payment above the monthly interest, or the card is never debt-free.

Does this calculator include fees or new purchases?

No. It assumes a fixed APR, no new spending on the card, no annual fee, no late fee and no balance-transfer fee — it models only the balance you enter being repaid month by month. In real life, new purchases add to the balance and stretch the payoff, and a rate change or a fee changes the result, so treat the figures as a plan for your current balance, not a promise from your card issuer.
