How to Calculate Your Due Date

Doctors count pregnancy from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP), not from the day of conception — because the LMP is a date most people can remember, while conception is usually about two weeks later and harder to pin down. The standard method is Naegele's rule: add 280 days, or 40 weeks, to the LMP. That gives the estimated due date, also called the estimated date of delivery (EDD). If your cycle is longer or shorter than 28 days, the date is shifted by the difference, since ovulation — and therefore conception — happens later or earlier in the cycle.

Your weeks pregnant are counted the same way: the number of whole weeks from your LMP to today. The Age Calculator uses the same idea of counting days between two dates, and the Date Calculator can add or subtract days from any date if you want to check a milestone by hand.

Pregnancy Formula

Due date = first day of last period + 280 days + (cycle length − 28 days)

Due date from conception = conception date + 266 days

Weeks pregnant = days from last period to today ÷ 7

Frequently Asked Questions

How is my due date calculated?

The due date is calculated with Naegele's rule: add 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP). If your cycle is longer or shorter than 28 days, the calculator adjusts the date by the difference — for example, a 30-day cycle moves the due date 2 days later. Enter your LMP above and the calculator does the maths instantly.

How many weeks pregnant am I?

Pregnancy is counted from the first day of your last menstrual period, not from conception. Count the days from your LMP to today and divide by 7: the whole number is your week of pregnancy and the remainder is the extra days. For example, 73 days after your LMP means you are 10 weeks and 3 days pregnant. The calculator above shows this automatically.

What are the trimesters of pregnancy?

Pregnancy is split into three trimesters: the first trimester runs from week 1 to week 13, the second trimester from week 14 to week 27, and the third trimester from week 28 until birth, usually around week 40. The calculator shows which trimester you are in now, based on your weeks pregnant.

What if I know my conception date instead?

If you know when conception happened, switch the calculator to the conception-date mode above. Your due date is then the conception date plus 266 days (38 weeks), because conception usually happens about 14 days after the last period. The calculator also works out your last period date from the conception date, so you still get your weeks pregnant and trimester.

Is the due date exact?

No — the due date is only an estimate. Only about 1 in 20 babies is born on the exact due date, and most arrive within two weeks either side of it. The date can also change after an early ultrasound, which measures the baby directly. This calculator gives an estimate only and is not medical advice: confirm your due date with your doctor or midwife.

What if my cycle is longer or shorter than 28 days?

Naegele's rule assumes a 28-day cycle, so the calculator adjusts for your real cycle length: it adds the difference between your cycle and 28 days to the due date. A 32-day cycle moves the due date 4 days later, and a 24-day cycle moves it 4 days earlier. If your cycles are irregular, the estimate is less reliable — an early ultrasound gives a more accurate date, so check with your doctor.
