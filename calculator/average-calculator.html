Average Formula

Mean = sum of all numbers ÷ count of numbers

Add up every number in the set, then divide by how many numbers there are. The median is the middle value of the sorted set (or the average of the two middle values when there is an even count), and the mode is the value that appears most often.

Worked example: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50. Sum = 150, count = 5, so mean = 150 ÷ 5 = 30. Sorted, the middle value is 30, so the median is also 30. Every value appears once, so there is no mode. Range = 50 − 10 = 40.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average of a set of numbers?

The average — also called the mean — is the sum of all the numbers divided by how many numbers there are. For example, the average of 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 is (10 + 20 + 30 + 40 + 50) ÷ 5 = 150 ÷ 5 = 30.

How is the median different from the mean?

The median is the middle value when the numbers are sorted in order, while the mean is the total divided by the count. For 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 the median is 30, the same as the mean here — but a very large or very small value pulls the mean away while the median stays in the middle.

What is the mode?

The mode is the number that appears most often in a set. For 5, 5, 10 and 15 the mode is 5, because it appears twice. If every number appears only once, there is no mode. A set can also have more than one mode.

When should I use the median instead of the mean?

Use the median when a few very large or very small values would distort the mean — for example with incomes or house prices, where a handful of huge values can make the average misleading. The median shows the typical value better in those cases.

Can I average negative or decimal numbers?

Yes. The same rules apply: add all the numbers (including negatives and decimals) and divide by how many there are. Just type the numbers separated by commas, spaces or new lines — for example, -5, 2.5, 10.
