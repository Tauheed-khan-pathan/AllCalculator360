How to Calculate BMR

BMR is the energy your body uses just to stay alive at complete rest — breathing, keeping your heart beating, maintaining body temperature and running your organs. It is the floor under everything else you burn in a day: any movement, digestion and exercise sit on top of it. Knowing it matters because every calorie plan, whether for losing or gaining weight, starts from this number.

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation estimates BMR from weight, height, age and sex, and it is the formula most dietitians use because it has held up best in studies. Men get a slightly higher result at the same size because, on average, they carry more muscle, which burns more calories at rest than fat does. BMR is not your total daily need, though — that is TDEE, which multiplies BMR by an activity factor. Work out your full daily figure with the Calorie Calculator, and check whether your weight sits in the healthy range with the BMI Calculator.

This is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the most widely used formula for estimating Basal Metabolic Rate — the calories your body burns at complete rest in a day.

BMR Formula

Men: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5

Women: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

BMR Calculator Example

Worked example: a 30-year-old man, 175 cm tall, weighing 70 kg: BMR = 10 × 70 + 6.25 × 175 − 5 × 30 + 5 = 700 + 1093.75 − 150 + 5 = 1648.75 ≈ 1649 kcal/day. That is about 11,541 kcal per week and 49,463 kcal per month at rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is BMR?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest in a day, just to keep you alive — breathing, keeping your heart beating, and maintaining your body temperature.

What is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is the most widely used formula for estimating BMR. For men it is 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5, and for women the same formula ends in − 161 instead of + 5.

Is BMR the same as my daily calorie needs?

No. BMR is only the calories you burn at rest. Your total daily calorie needs (TDEE) are higher, because they also include the calories you burn through daily activity and exercise.

Why is BMR higher for men than women?

On average men have more muscle mass, and muscle burns more calories at rest than fat. That is why the Mifflin-St Jeor equation adds 5 for men and subtracts 161 for women.

Can I use my BMR to lose weight?

Yes. Eating fewer calories than your total daily needs creates a calorie deficit, which leads to weight loss over time. Use the Calorie Calculator to find your daily target. This calculator is for information only and is not medical advice.

Does BMR change as I get older?

Yes. BMR falls slowly with age, roughly 5 calories per year in the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, because muscle mass tends to decrease and the body becomes more efficient. Staying active and keeping muscle through strength training is the best way to keep your BMR higher as you age.
