How to Calculate EMI

To calculate EMI by hand, first turn the annual interest rate into a monthly rate by dividing it by 12 and then by 100. Next, turn the loan tenure from years into months. Those two numbers — the monthly rate r and the number of months n — go into the formula below along with the loan amount P.

The formula works because the lender charges interest on the balance that is still outstanding, and that balance shrinks a little with every payment. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal. That is why a longer tenure feels cheaper month to month but costs more overall — compare the total interest, not just the EMI, before you sign. The same maths works for any loan: the Loan Calculator shows it for a general loan, and the Mortgage Calculator adds property tax and insurance to the monthly cost.

EMI Formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P = Loan amount (principal)  •  r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)  •  n = Number of monthly instalments (tenure in months)

EMI Example

Here is the EMI formula applied to a typical home loan, step by step.

Worked example: Loan of 1,000,000 at 9% annual interest for 20 years (240 months): r = 0.09 ÷ 12 = 0.0075, n = 240, EMI = 1,000,000 × 0.0075 × (1.0075)240 ÷ ((1.0075)240 − 1) = 8,997 per month (8,997.26 exact). Total payment = 8,997.26 × 240 = 2,159,342; total interest = 2,159,342 − 1,000,000 = 1,159,342.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment: the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan, covering both principal and interest.

How is EMI calculated?

EMI is calculated with the formula EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), from the loan amount, the annual interest rate and the tenure. A higher rate or longer tenure raises total interest; a longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI but increases total interest.

Does prepayment reduce my interest?

Yes. Paying extra towards the principal reduces the outstanding balance, so future interest is charged on a smaller amount — this shortens the tenure or lowers the EMI.

What happens if I miss an EMI payment?

The lender usually charges a late fee, and it can lower your credit score, which makes future loans costlier.

Can my EMI change during the loan?

With a fixed interest rate it stays the same; with a floating rate the EMI (or tenure) changes when the lender revises the rate.

How can I reduce my EMI?

A lower interest rate or a larger down payment reduces your EMI, because both shrink the amount you borrow or the rate charged on it. A shorter tenure raises the monthly EMI but cuts the total interest you pay, while a longer tenure does the opposite. Making part-prepayments whenever you have spare cash also brings the balance — and the interest — down.
