Discount Calculator Formula

Final price = original price × (1 − discount% ÷ 100)

Amount saved = original price − final price

Worked example: A $100 item at 25% off costs $100 × (1 − 25 ÷ 100) = $100 × 0.75 = $75, and you save $100 − $75 = $25.

Original price = discounted price ÷ (1 − discount% ÷ 100)

Worked example: An item costs $75 after a 25% discount, so the original price was $75 ÷ (1 − 25 ÷ 100) = $75 ÷ 0.75 = $100, and you saved $100 − $75 = $25.

Discount % = (original price − final price) ÷ original price × 100

Worked example: A $100 item marked down to $75 is discounted by ($100 − $75) ÷ $100 × 100 = 25% off.

Discounts do not add up when stacked: a 20% discount followed by 10% off is not 30% off. The second discount applies to the already-discounted price, so you pay 80% × 90% = 72% of the original price — that is 28% off in total.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a discount?

Multiply the original price by (1 − discount percentage ÷ 100). For example, a $100 item at 25% off costs $100 × (1 − 25 ÷ 100) = $100 × 0.75 = $75, so you save $25.

How do I find the original price from a sale price?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount percentage ÷ 100). For example, if an item costs $75 after a 25% discount, the original price was $75 ÷ (1 − 25 ÷ 100) = $75 ÷ 0.75 = $100, so you saved $25.

What does "25% off" mean?

It means you pay 75% of the original price and save 25%. On a $100 item, 25% off means you pay $75 and save $25.

How are taxes handled on a discounted price?

Tax is usually charged on the discounted price, after the discount is applied — so you pay tax on the sale price, not on the original price. The exact rule depends on where you live, so check your local tax rules if you are unsure.

Can I stack two discounts?

No — two discounts do not simply add up. A 20% discount followed by a 10% discount is not 30% off; the second discount applies to the already-discounted price, so you pay 80% × 90% = 72% of the original price, which is 28% off the original.
