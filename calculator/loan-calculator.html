Loan Calculator Formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P = Loan amount (principal)  •  r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)  •  n = Number of monthly instalments (tenure in months)

Total payment = EMI × n

Total interest = total payment − loan amount

Each monthly payment has two parts: interest on the outstanding balance, and principal that reduces the balance. Early payments are mostly interest; as the balance falls, more of each payment goes towards principal. That is why the amortisation schedule above shows interest falling and principal rising year by year.

Worked example: A $100,000 loan at 8% annual interest for 20 years (240 months): r = 0.08 ÷ 12 = 0.006667, n = 240, monthly payment = 100,000 × 0.006667 × (1.006667)240 ÷ ((1.006667)240 − 1) = $836.44 per month. Total payment = $836.44 × 240 = $200,746 ($200,745.62 exact); total interest = $200,745.62 − $100,000 = $100,746 ($100,745.62 exact).

Second example: A $10,000 loan at 6% annual interest for 5 years (60 months): monthly payment = $193.33 per month. Total payment = $193.33 × 60 = $11,600; total interest = $11,600 − $10,000 = $1,600.

Note: this calculation does not include processing fees, insurance or taxes — see the FAQ below.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment: the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan, covering both principal and interest. Each payment is split into interest on the outstanding balance and principal that reduces the balance, so the loan is fully repaid by the end of the tenure.

What is the difference between fixed and reducing-balance interest?

With reducing-balance interest, interest is charged only on the outstanding loan balance, so it falls as you repay the loan — this is how almost all home, car and personal loans work. With a flat rate, interest is charged on the full original loan amount for the whole tenure, so the same nominal rate costs you much more in total. Always compare loans on the reducing-balance rate.

How does tenure affect my monthly payment?

A longer tenure spreads the loan over more months, so the monthly payment falls — but you pay interest for longer, so the total interest rises. A shorter tenure raises the monthly payment but cuts the total interest you pay. For example, a $100,000 loan at 8% costs about $836 a month over 20 years but about $1,559 a month over 7 years, with far less total interest.

Should I prepay my loan?

Prepaying usually saves money: every extra payment goes straight towards the principal, so the outstanding balance falls faster and future interest is charged on a smaller amount. That shortens the loan or lowers the remaining payments and cuts the total interest. Check with your lender first, though — some loans charge a prepayment fee, so make sure the interest you save is more than the fee.

What is amortisation?

Amortisation is the schedule showing how each monthly payment is split between interest and principal over the life of the loan. Early payments are mostly interest, because the balance is at its highest; as the balance falls, more of each payment goes towards principal. The yearly table on this page shows that split, and the final balance is $0 when the loan is fully repaid.
