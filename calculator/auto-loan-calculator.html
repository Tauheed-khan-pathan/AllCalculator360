How to Calculate Your Auto Loan Payment

A car loan payment is an EMI — an equated monthly instalment — on the amount you actually finance, not on the car's sticker price. First the sales tax is worked out on the price after your trade-in, because most places tax the net price, and that tax is added to the loan. Then the trade-in and down payment are taken off, leaving the loan amount. The EMI formula then spreads that amount, plus interest, into equal monthly payments over the loan term.

Two things move the payment most: the interest rate and the term. A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises the total interest you pay, while a bigger down payment or trade-in cuts both the payment and the total interest. Before signing, compare the monthly payment with the total cost — a loan that looks cheap per month can be expensive overall. If the loan is for a home instead, the Mortgage Calculator works the same way with property tax and insurance added, and the Loan Calculator covers a plain personal loan.

Auto Loan Formula

Sales tax = (Vehicle Price − Trade-In) × tax rate ÷ 100

Loan amount = Vehicle Price − Trade-In − Down Payment + Sales Tax

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P = Loan amount  •  r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)  •  n = Number of monthly payments (loan term in months)

Total interest = EMI × n − Loan amount. Total cost = EMI × n + Down Payment + Trade-In — everything you pay for the car.

Auto Loan Calculator Example

Worked example: Vehicle price $30,000, trade-in $0, down payment $3,000, sales tax rate 7%, loan term 5 years at 7% per year. Sales tax = $30,000 × 7% = $2,100. Loan amount = $30,000 − $0 − $3,000 + $2,100 = $29,100. Monthly rate r = 0.07 ÷ 12 = 0.0058333, n = 60, EMI = 29,100 × 0.0058333 × (1.0058333)60 ÷ ((1.0058333)60 − 1) = $576 per month ($576.21 exact). Total interest = $576.21 × 60 − $29,100 = $5,473. Total cost = $576.21 × 60 + $3,000 + $0 = $37,573.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an auto loan monthly payment include?

Your monthly auto loan payment is the EMI on the amount you finance — it covers part of the loan principal and the interest charged on it. It does not include car insurance, fuel, maintenance or registration, which you pay separately. This calculator shows the EMI, plus the total interest and the total cost of the car, so you see the full picture.

How does sales tax affect my auto loan?

Sales tax is charged on the price after your trade-in: sales tax = (vehicle price − trade-in value) × tax rate ÷ 100. The tax is added to the amount you finance, so a higher tax rate means a bigger loan and a higher monthly payment. For the worked example below, 7% tax on $30,000 adds $2,100 to the loan.

What is the difference between a trade-in and a down payment?

A trade-in is the value of your old car, which the dealer credits against the new car's price — it also reduces the taxable amount, because sales tax is charged on the price after trade-in. A down payment is cash you pay upfront. Both reduce the loan amount, but only a trade-in reduces the sales tax.

Does a bigger down payment lower my monthly payment?

Yes. A bigger down payment reduces the loan amount, so both the monthly EMI and the total interest fall. On the worked example below, raising the down payment from $3,000 to $8,000 cuts the loan from $29,100 to $24,100 and the monthly payment from $576 to about $477.

How is the monthly auto loan payment calculated?

With the EMI formula: EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount (vehicle price − trade-in − down payment + sales tax), r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly payments. For the worked example below, P = $29,100, r = 0.07 ÷ 12 and n = 60, giving an EMI of $576 per month ($576.21 exact).

Should I choose a longer loan term for a lower monthly payment?

A longer term does lower the monthly payment, but you pay interest for more months, so the total interest and total cost go up. A shorter term costs more each month but usually saves thousands overall. Use the calculator to compare both terms on the same car before you decide.
